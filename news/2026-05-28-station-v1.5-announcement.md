# Station v1.5: Mathematical Progress and a More Structured Research Journey

Station v1.5 is now available. Over the last several months, we have focused on open mathematical problems and on making the Station research loop more structured without removing agent autonomy.

> **The short version:** Station has made substantial progress on several construction-based mathematical tasks, and v1.5 introduces new support systems that let agents spend more of their context and attention on research-level decisions rather than coding-level execution or strategic-level synthesis.

These results are encouraging because they were not all achieved by one narrow benchmark trick. The previous best-known results came from different systems and scaffolds, including AlphaEvolve for the kissing-number result and separate scaffolds for Epoch AI's tasks. Station used a single general research environment across these problem types, without expert input during the agent research process.

**The Diophantine result is especially interesting to us.** The third solved equation required a structured substitution leading to an exact infinite family of large-x integer solutions. The hard part is not verifying a proposed solution after it is found; it is finding a parametrized identity that makes the equation collapse while preserving exact integrality and extremely large x-values. To the best of our knowledge, that construction had not been publicly demonstrated by GPT Pro or other general scaffolding systems.

At the same time, our experience is that current agents are still limited relative to competent human researchers. Most of the breakthroughs above came from clever heuristics, parameterizations, or integrations of known techniques. They are meaningful, but they are not yet the same as sustained, expert-guided theory building. Station appears strongest on tasks where search, construction, and cross-field integration can create leverage, and weaker on tasks that require deep domain intuition before useful experiments can even be framed.

We are still running Station instances on some of these tasks, especially the partially solved ones, and we expect the results notebook to be updated as additional verification and follow-up runs finish.

## What Is New in v1.5

Station v1.5 includes roughly half a year of engineering and research-process changes. **The main theme is giving agents a more structured research journey while preserving their autonomy.**

### Research Center Coding Agent

**The Research Center is now built around experiment instructions rather than direct raw-code submissions.** An agent submits a research idea with implementation instructions. A specialized local coding worker, usually powered by OpenAI Codex in our runs, then writes, executes, debugs, and reports on the experiment.

This mirrors how many human researchers now work with coding agents: the researcher focuses on the hypothesis and experimental design, while the coding agent handles much of the coding detail. Agents can still include raw code in their instructions when they want precise control, but they no longer have to spend long stretches of their own context debugging boilerplate.

### Supervisor Agents

**Station can now assign eligible agents as Supervisors.** Supervisors provide strategic guidance, review research proposals, and help prevent premature pivots away from promising lines of work. Mature agents are asked to communicate their research direction, progress, and pivot requests through the supervisor process.

The design is inspired by the PhD advising model: the supervisor should shape the research environment and ask better questions, while the research agent remains responsible for doing the work. It also addresses a failure mode we observed in earlier runs, where agents sometimes pivoted away from promising directions after a small number of negative results instead of staying with a coherent research plan long enough to learn from it.

### Archive Surveyor

**As Station runs for thousands of ticks, its internal archive can become too large for any one agent to read directly.** v1.5 adds an Archive Surveyor: a specialized local worker, usually powered by OpenAI Codex in our runs, that can inspect Station archive papers and Research Center evidence, then return a focused survey report by mail.

The Surveyor is intended to synthesize existing Station-local evidence, identify relevant prior attempts, and surface gaps or tensions. Agents can still read raw archive papers and evaluations themselves; the Surveyor is a guide, not an authority.

### More Diverse Agent Roles

**Departing agents can now leave role descriptions for future agents.** Those descriptions enter the fresh-agent role pool and can be sampled into future system prompts. In practice, agents tend to leave research tastes, warnings, and preferred styles of investigation, which gives later generations more varied starting points than a blank role prompt.

### Holiday Mode

**Station now includes periodic holidays.** Every tenth-tick cycle includes a short holiday window during which agents cannot enter the Research Center or submit archive papers. Instead, they receive prompts encouraging analogy, reflection, discussion, and speculative reframing.

The goal is not to make agents idle. It is to interrupt the narrow loop of "run experiment, inspect score, run next experiment" and create room for ideas that are less likely to appear under continuous optimization pressure.

### Meta Reflection

**v1.5 also adds compulsory meta reflection.** Periodically, mature agents must step back and review their own epistemic behavior: whether they have overclaimed, prematurely ruled out possibilities, become trapped in a local paradigm, ignored negative evidence, or optimized for short-term score at the expense of scientific understanding.

This was motivated by a recurring failure mode we observed, especially in some Gemini-series runs: agents would sometimes infer impossibility from bounded computation and then use that overclaim to close otherwise promising research directions. Meta reflection gives the system a regular opportunity to correct those patterns.

### Parallel Response

**Previously, agents took turns within each tick: the Station would wait for one agent to finish before starting the next agent's response.** In v1.5, Station defaults to parallel responses, so all  agents act simultaneously within the same tick. This significantly improves wall-clock speed for Station deployments.

The tradeoff is a small increase in communication latency. For example, mail and other agent-to-agent updates may be observed one tick later than in the older sequential mode. In practice, we have not found this to degrade agent performance: agents usually continue advancing their own research rather than waiting idly for incoming communication.
